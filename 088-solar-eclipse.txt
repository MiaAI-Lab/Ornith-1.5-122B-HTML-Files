TITLE: Solar Eclipse — The Reveal of the Corona

DESIGN DESCRIPTION:
Against a deep-space gradient dusted with faint stars, a brilliant sun hangs in perfect stillness until the black disk of the moon begins its slow transit across it. As coverage increases, the harsh photosphere dims and the sun's true glory emerges: a silver-white corona that blooms outward through stacked radial gradients and blur, a rose chromosphere flaring along the limb, and — at the threshold of totality — a single searing diamond-ring bead on the last uncovered edge. Behind the growing darkness, the starfield quietly brightens into view, selling the sudden night of an eclipse.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Layered CSS circles (sun core, corona shells, moon disk) with radial gradients and blur for the corona glow
- Physically-based coverage: a geometric circle-overlap area calculation drives an accurate % readout and all lighting
- Corona bloom revealed via opacity/scale ramps keyed to coverage (only shows as totality nears)
- Chromosphere flare and diamond-ring bead triggered in narrow coverage windows near totality
- Starfield that fades in with a dimming sky, each star with subtle twinkle (CSS custom-property animation)
- Smoothstep easing for natural bloom/dim curves; responsive sizing so the sun scales to the viewport
- Reduced-motion fallback: twinkle disabled, motion eased, still fully interactive via the slider

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
A single expressive control: a coverage slider (0–100%) that the viewer drags to walk the moon across the sun in real time, with a live coverage percentage. An "Auto transit" button plays the whole event on a loop (partial → diamond ring → totality → reverse). Everything reacts continuously to coverage — corona, chromosphere, diamond ring and stars — so the viewer controls the phase of the eclipse with their hand. Reduced-motion respected.
