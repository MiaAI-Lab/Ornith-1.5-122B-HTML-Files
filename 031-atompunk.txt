TITLE:
ATOMIC AGE — Welcome to the Space Race

DESIGN DESCRIPTION:
A portrait poster rendered entirely as flat, mid-century vector illustration, framed like a museum print. A warm cream sky opens behind a radial sunburst of mustard and orange rays, scattered twinkling stars and 4-point atomic sparkles. At the heart sits an "atomic planet": a teal sphere with a glowing nucleus of clustered protons and neutrons, ringed by three tilted orbital ellipses whose electrons race around it. A finned rocket climbs on flickering gold flames at a diagonal, while a domed flying saucer drifts with blinking running lights. Bold, tightly-tracked display type reads "ATOMIC AGE" with a soft orange offset shadow, and a deep-teal footer banner carries a serif italic tagline — "Est. MCMLV" — completing the optimistic 1950s space-race fantasy.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Single inline <svg viewBox="0 0 1000 1400"> poster held in a fixed aspect-ratio frame that scales fluidly from 320px to 4K with no external assets.
- Flat geometric shapes (polygons, circles, ellipses, gradient-filled paths) in a curated teal / orange / cream / mustard palette with offset "letterpress" drop-shadow type.
- Orbiting electrons use SMIL <animateMotion> + <mpath> along hidden ellipse paths, while the visible orbit rings spin via CSS, giving true elliptical travel.
- CSS keyframe motion for the spinning sunburst, floating rocket & saucer, flickering exhaust, blinking saucer lights, pulsing nucleus and randomly-delayed twinkling stars/sparkles.
- Procedurally generated in JS: the sunburst rays, a field of stars (kept clear of the atom), and 4-point sparkle polygons — so the composition feels hand-populated yet organic.
- Multi-layer pointer parallax (sunburst, stars, scene) driven by a smooth requestAnimationFrame loop with an idle sine drift when untouched, and a prefers-reduced-motion branch that halts all motion.

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
Quietly cinematic and pointer / touch driven, with no buttons or controls. Moving the cursor (or dragging on touch) tilts the poster in 3D-ish parallax — the sunburst, stars and foreground scene shift at different depths, as if the print is catching the light. When left untouched, the whole scene performs a slow, continuous idle drift and the atom keeps spinning, the rocket keeps climbing, and stars keep twinkling on their own. Motion is deliberately gentle and looping so it reads as a living screen-saver poster; on prefers-reduced-motion the parallax and all animation stop, leaving the full flat illustration intact and legible.
