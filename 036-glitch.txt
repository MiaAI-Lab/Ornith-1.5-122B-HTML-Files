TITLE:
GL1TCH — Datamoshed Portrait

DESIGN DESCRIPTION:
A corrupted-broadcast portrait on pure black. An inline-SVG geometric face is drawn three times and layered with additive blending — a white base plus cyan and magenta copies offset by a few pixels — producing a constant anaglyph RGB split. Over the portrait, eight clip-path horizontal bands (datamosh slices) each hold their own shifted copy of the face and are randomly jolted sideways on a fast interval, so the image tears itself apart and reassembles. Every few frames the whole composition jumps and flashes (brightness/contrast burst); the headline "GL1TCH" in a heavy grotesque is triple-layered and periodically explodes its channels; a mono HUD readout (REC · 036, CH-NOISE / RGB) flickers between random hex frames; scanlines and a slow-moving highlight band sweep the screen; a vignette and HUD corner brackets frame it like a damaged monitor.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- clip-path: inset(...) on eight stacked horizontal bands, each containing a shifted SVG copy, for the datamosh tear effect (with -webkit- prefix).
- RGB channel offset via three stacked layers (base white + cyan/magenta) using mix-blend-mode: screen for additive chromatic separation on black.
- A single inline <symbol id="face"> reused through <use href> / xlink:href so the portrait is duplicated cheaply across base, RGB and slice layers.
- requestAnimationFrame-free randomised datamosh loop (setInterval ~130ms) translating slice layers by random px; full-frame jump + brightness/contrast flash on a slower interval.
- CSS scanlines (repeating-linear-gradient, multiply blend) plus an animated highlight sweep, radial vignette, and CSS HUD corner brackets / barcode (repeating-linear-gradient).
- Blinking REC dot and status cursor via steps() keyframes; prefers-reduced-motion disables all motion and the intervals, leaving the static RGB-split composition.

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
Passive, ambient — a self-corrupting "broken signal" wall. Nothing is clickable; the portrait continuously datamoshes, jumps and re-glitches on its own on a randomized cadence, adapting to the OS reduced-motion setting.
