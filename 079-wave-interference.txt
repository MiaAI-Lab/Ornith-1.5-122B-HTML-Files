TITLE:
RIPPLE — A Field of Two Voices

DESIGN DESCRIPTION:
Over a deep-water gradient that falls from midnight blue to near-black, two coherent point sources pulse in opposition and throw concentric ripples that cross, reinforce and cancel across the whole field. The interference is computed pixel-by-pixel as the sum of two sinusoids (amplitude fall-off 1/√r) and color-mapped through a cold palette — abyssal navy in the nodes, teal and cyan on the rising crests, glacial white at the anti-nodes — so the classic hyperbolic pattern of constructive and destructive bands glows like sonar. Two pulsing source markers sit at the heart of the field, a faint grain and vignette add depth, and a glass control panel lets you reshape the physics live: frequency, source spacing, a phase toggle between in-phase and anti-phase (which rotates the entire nodal pattern), and a speed dial. The whole thing reads as a scientific instrument rendered as fine art.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Per-pixel interference computed by summing two distance-based sinusoids (A = sin(k·r1 − ωt)/√r1 + sin(k·r2 − ωt + φ)/√r2) over an offscreen low-resolution buffer, then ImageData-set for speed.
- Cold color-map built as a 256-entry gradient through navy → deep blue → teal → cyan → pale cyan → near-white, applied by mapping normalized amplitude to a palette index.
- Live animation via a requestAnimationFrame delta-time loop; the field re-solves each frame so the ripples visibly travel outward and the bands breathe.
- Optional static (reduce-motion) mode that solves the field once and freezes it, plus a low-DPR cap so the pixel pass stays cheap on 4K.
- Pulsing source markers drawn as layered radial gradients (glow halo + bright core) with a shared sine phase, and a subtle per-frame radial vignette plus a one-shot grain overlay for a water-texture feel.
- Glassmorphic control panel (backdrop-filter blur, translucent fill, fine border) with a three-stop colour legend and live readouts of the current parameters.

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
Pointer / touch driven. Drag anywhere on the field to reposition the interference pattern (both sources translate together, keeping their spacing), or drag directly on a source marker to move it. Three sliders reshape the physics in real time — Frequency (wave number, how tightly the bands are packed), Spacing (distance between the two sources, which sets the band angular density) and Speed (temporal rate), with a Speed dial that also carries an anti-phase toggle that flips the phase difference by π and rotates the nodal hyperbolas. Everything animates continuously; prefers-reduced-motion solves a single static frame so the pattern is still fully legible without motion.
