TITLE:
PELAGIA — Stratification of the Sea

DESIGN DESCRIPTION:
A full-bleed vertical water column descends from sunlit teal to abyssal black, rendered as a single multi-stop gradient that encodes the ocean's real thermal structure. Four wavy SVG "water-mass" bands drift horizontally at their own speeds to make the stratification physically legible as stacked, moving layers, while god rays, a shimmering surface caustic and a fine SVG-noise grain add atmosphere. Inline-SVG creatures inhabit their correct zones — pulsing translucent jellyfish with swaying tentacles in the mesopelagic, a bobbing school of lanternfish with blinking bioluminescent lures in the bathypelagic, a golden reef fish near the surface, and a near-invisible leviathan silhouette in the abyss — all drifting on their own CSS currents. A right-hand depth scale ticks through Epipelagic → Hadal with depth and temperature, a live surface-temperature readout flickers, and a warm-to-cold gradient legend ties the whole piece together as a curated scientific data-art panel.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- A single 10-stop vertical gradient (bright teal → abyss black) doubles as both the scene and the temperature data, so the art IS the chart.
- Four parallax stratified bands built from wavy inline-SVG paths filled with depth-appropriate translucent teals/blues, each on its own infinite translateX drift animation (46s–110s) so the layering reads as moving water masses.
- Creatures drawn entirely as inline SVG with per-part CSS animation: a jellyfish bell that pulses (scaleY/scaleX), tentacles that sway (skewX), a fish school that "bobs" and flaps fins, and bioluminescent lures that glow on staggered timers.
- Layered atmosphere: animated god rays (repeating-linear-gradient with a vertical mask), a screen-blended surface caustic, and a subtle feTurbulence grain overlay for a filmic, printed-data feel.
- A lightweight <canvas> "marine snow" field (devicePixelRatio-scaled, capped 2×) of slowly settling, swaying particles driven by a scroll-controlled upward current.
- Typographic system pairing a Georgia serif display headline with mono metadata (depths, temperatures, readouts) and a warm→cold gradient legend bar; responsive rules hide the scale below 600px and prefers-reduced-motion freezes all animation.

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
Ambient and pointer/scroll driven, no buttons. The scene is always alive — creatures drift on independent loops and marine snow settles continuously. Scrolling (or trackpad swipe) injects a brief upward current that stirs the snow and water, then relaxes back to rest, as if a submersible's thruster disturbed the column. The surface temperature readout drifts gently on its own so the panel feels instrumented and breathing. Everything is CSS-animation-led for zero-jank idle motion, and prefers-reduced-motion collapses the whole piece to a still, contemplative frame.
