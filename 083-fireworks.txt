TITLE
-----
Fireworks — A Night Display

DESIGN DESCRIPTION
------------------
A full-bleed canvas night sky, deep navy and dusted with softly twinkling stars, where an autonomous display of rockets climbs and bursts overhead. Each rocket is a glowing ember that ascends under decelerating gravity, leaving a tapering spark trail, and at its apex detonates into a radial fireball of gravity-affected, friction-damped particles in a curated multicolor palette — rose, gold, emerald, azure, violet — with a bright central flash and a scatter of short-lived white-gold crackle sparks that flicker and die. The whole scene is painted with additive blending and a translucent per-frame fade so every streak leaves a luminous, smoky trail. Rockets launch on their own at randomized intervals, and the viewer can click or tap anywhere to send a rocket up toward that point, bursting in their chosen colours.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES
-----------------------
- Canvas 2D physics: per-particle gravity, air friction, randomized launch angle/speed and individual life/decay for organic, physics-driven explosions.
- Rocket ascent + burst: embers rise with a decelerating vertical velocity, burst at apex (when velocity crosses zero) into a radial particle fireball plus a central radial-gradient flash.
- Fading trails: a translucent dark fill (rgba 6,8,18 @0.22) each frame instead of a clear, so streaks smoulder and fade like real pyrotechnics; additive (lighter) blending for glow.
- Crackle sparkle: a secondary ring of fast, short-lived near-white sparks with thin strokes for the classic gold crackle finish; occasional all-gold bursts.
- Curated multicolor palette via an HSL hue set, each particle nudged ±7° for natural variation; DPR-aware canvas scaling for crisp lines on retina.
- Twinkling starfield background sized to the viewport; responsive full-screen canvas 320px to 4K.
- prefers-reduced-motion: a single quiet volley then hold, no continuous auto-launch loop.

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL
--------------------------
Dual-mode: passively watch the auto-generated display, or take control by clicking / tapping anywhere on the sky to launch a rocket aimed at that spot — the burst happens at the click's height. Rapid clicking fires multiple rockets. On touch devices the pointer still works. Motion is continuous and celebratory; reduced-motion collapses it to one restrained volley. No UI chrome beyond a minimal title and a single instruction line.
