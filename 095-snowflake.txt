PROMPT:
Create a single, fully self-contained HTML5 page (all CSS and JavaScript inline, no
CDN, no external fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no libraries)
titled "Hexapod — a crystal that grows itself". It is a crystallography showcase:
a deep-icy-blue stage on which a fresh, six-fold snowflake dendrite grows itself
from a single seed of ice. Pressing a button generates a brand-new procedurally
unique crystal — no two alike — that draws its branches on line by line, from the
centre outward, six times (once per symmetry arm). The mood is precise, cold and
scientific yet delicate, like a museum exhibit of frozen light. Make it beautiful,
responsive and offline-capable; the whole experience must live inside one file.

TITLE:
Hexapod — a crystal that grows itself (Crystallography · No. 095)

DESIGN DESCRIPTION:
A tall, centered composition on a layered radial-gradient backdrop that sinks from
a pale arctic glow at the top to a near-black midnight blue at the edges, sprinkled
with faint drifting frost dust (a fixed pseudo-element of tiny radial-gradient
"stars" that slowly rises). A serif display headline ("Hexapod") renders in a
white-to-cyan vertical gradient with background-clip:text, above a muted caption.
The hero is a square stage (aspect-ratio 1:1, capped at 78vh) holding one inline
SVG (viewBox 0 0 1000 1000) with a soft radial bloom behind the crystal. The
snowflake itself is built from hundreds of thin gradient-stroked lines (a white→
ice-blue→cyan linear gradient), each with rounded caps, arranged in six rotated
copies for perfect six-fold symmetry, plus a small central hexagonal hub drawn last.
Around it, 24–48 tiny six-point star sparkles twinkle on and off at staggered rates.
Controls sit beneath: a primary "Grow a new crystal" button, a "Slow spin" toggle,
and a tabular-numeral meta readout ("arms · 6 · depth 4"). A spaced uppercase
footer credit closes the page. The palette is ice (#dff3ff), cyan (#7fd0ff), frost
(#5aa9e6) and pale blue on deep navy; glassy translucent buttons with backdrop-blur
reinforce the frozen-glass feel.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Pure-JS recursive dendrite generator: a `branch()` routine sprouts two symmetric
  side-branches at each tip, occasional forward sub-branches for lushness, and a
  flat hexagonal tip pair, capped so no arm exceeds a segment budget.
- Six-fold symmetry by rotating every segment 6×60° with a hand-rolled 2D rotation
  transform, so one generated arm becomes a complete crystal.
- Stroke-draw animation: each line sets strokeDasharray/strokeDashoffset to its own
  length and animates dashoffset to 0 over ~2.5s with a staggered per-segment delay,
  so the crystal visibly "grows" outward from the hub.
- A central hexagonal polygon hub that draws on last, plus a radial bloom gradient
  for a soft icy glow (a Gaussian-blur halo filter is declared in <defs> but left
  unused). Scattered around the crystal, 24–48 tiny six-point star sparkles are
  generated as star paths and twinkle on/off via a CSS scale+opacity keyframe at
  staggered random delays, filling the space between arms with drifting light.
- CSS: layered radial-gradient background, drifting frost-dust pseudo-element,
  gradient-clipped headline text, glassmorphic buttons, tabular-numeral meta,
  responsive clamp() typography and a mobile breakpoint that hides the meta line.

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
The page is contemplative and generative. On load a crystal appears and, if "Slow
spin" is checked, the whole assembly rotates very slowly. The core action is the
"Grow a new crystal" button: each press wipes the stage and regenerates a new random
parameter set (spine length, branch depth 4 or 5, spread angle, taper, tip-bar
chance), then re-runs the outward stroke-draw growth so the viewer watches a
genuinely new, never-before-seen snowflake assemble itself. The spin toggle starts/
stops the rotation; the meta readout updates to report the new arm count and branch
depth. Everything works offline, is responsive from phone to desktop, and degrades
gracefully.
