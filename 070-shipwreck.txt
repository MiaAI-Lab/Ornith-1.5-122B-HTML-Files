TITLE:
------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABYSS · The Wreck at Dusk

------------------------------------------------------------------------
DESIGN DESCRIPTION:
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A ruined hull lies slumped on the seabed in deep teal and murky green,
while pale god-rays spear down from the distant surface and scatter
through the water column. Marine snow drifts endlessly past, strands of
seaweed sway with the current, and a school of fish parts and reforms as
it passes through the light. The scene is quiet, heavy and vast — a
place of slow motion and filtered, sacred light.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Canvas volumetric god-rays: overlapping translucent gradient polygons
  that rotate slowly and shimmer to read as shafts of light in water.
- Particle field of drifting marine snow rendered on canvas with parallax
  depth (near/far layers at different speeds and opacities).
- Seaweed drawn as layered bezier fronds with a sine-driven sway
  animation anchored at the base and amplifying toward the tips.
- A school of fish as small lens-flare-shaped sprites that follow a
  curved path, spawn and despawn off-screen, and drift through the rays.
- Radial vignette and a deep teal->green vertical gradient for depth.
- Foreground silhouette props (coral, hull fragments, a drifting jelly)
  to frame the composition and add foreground/mid/background layers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ambient and self-running — the wreck breathes on its own with rays
shifting, snow drifting and seaweed swaying. The pointer acts as a gentle
current: moving it nudges the snow and fish away from the cursor and
bends the light, and a click releases a rising bubble. Interaction is
soft and non-destructive, preserving the meditative underwater mood.
