TITLE
-----
Chrome · A Floating Studio Sphere

DESIGN DESCRIPTION
------------------
A single, flawless chrome sphere hangs in the dead centre of a bright cyclorama studio, drifting up and down in a slow, weightless float while its mirror surface reflects a softbox-lit environment. The sphere's believable metal look is built from stacked CSS gradients: a radial body shading from a bright top-left light down to a dark lower-right, an inset highlight and shadow for roundness, a slowly rotating conic-gradient disc that reads as reflected bands of light and grey, and a bright softbox streak that drifts across the surface. A crisp, pointer-reactive specular highlight and a thin lower rim light complete the illusion, while a soft blurred contact shadow on the "floor" breathes in counter-phase to the float to sell the height. The palette is pure mirror chrome against soft white/grey. A minimal, wide-tracked caption anchors the composition.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES
-----------------------
- Believable chrome from layered CSS gradients: a radial body gradient (bright top-left → dark lower-right) plus inset box-shadows for specular core and form shadow — no images, no WebGL.
- Reflection via a large conic-gradient disc (inset beyond the circle) rotating slowly under mix-blend-mode:screen, so light/grey bands shimmer like a turning mirror.
- Animated environment bands: the spinning conic reflection plus a drifting linear-gradient "softbox streak" for living, moving reflections.
- Soft contact shadow: a blurred radial ellipse that scales and fades in counter-phase to the float, reinforcing the sphere's height.
- Subtle float: a single cubic-bezier translateY loop on the scene.
- Pointer-reactive specular highlight: a small radial highlight repositioned toward the cursor via CSS custom properties (--hx, --hy) so the light appears to follow the viewer.
- Responsive sizing with clamp()/vmin scaling 320px to 4K; full prefers-reduced-motion fallback (freezes float, reflection and drift); touch screens show the sphere at a fixed studio light angle.

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL
--------------------------
Mostly contemplative and passive: the sphere floats and reflects on its own. Moving the pointer (or device) sweeps the specular highlight across the surface — "move to catch the light" — giving a tactile sense of a real reflective object. Motion is calm and looped; when reduced motion is requested everything holds still at a fixed, well-lit angle. No navigation — the frame is the studio.
